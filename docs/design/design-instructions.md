Grounding: built from the repo at `yhaspel/ZenPDF@main` (frontend: Angular 22 + Tailwind CSS v4). **Every affordance specified here maps to an action that exists in the codebase today** — that is the rule, and it is the one that matters. - **2026-08-23** — the **`image_stamp` entry in the annotate palette**: not a new capability but the palette entry the sixteenth tool never had, so the tool an upload arms can be seen and returned to.

## 1. Design language

### Concept — Zen ink & paper
ZenPDF is a quiet desk: warm paper surfaces, ink text, and one vermilion seal that marks the moments that matter. The name finally means something: calm, not decoration. Emptiness (ma) is deliberate — a screen should feel like a well-set page, not a dashboard.

### Principles
1. **Paper, not gray.** Every surface is a warm paper tone. Never blue-slate, never `#FFFFFF` as a page background (pure white is reserved for rendered PDF pages, which must read as *paper on the desk*).
2. **Ink, not black.** Hierarchy comes from size, weight and space — color is almost never the differentiator.
3. **One accent, used like a seal.** Vermilion (`--color-accent`) appears only on: the logo seal, the single primary action of a screen, focus rings, selection marks, and completion stamps. If vermilion is on screen twice with no reason, that is a bug to fix.
4. **Ma.** Section gaps on marketing pages are `--space-8`/`--space-9`. Do not fill space; compose it.
5. **Calm motion.** 150–300 ms, `cubic-bezier(.4,0,.2,1)`, garnish only. Everything must be fully usable with animation removed (the global `prefers-reduced-motion` collapse stays).

### Signature motifs
- **The seal (logo mark).** A vermilion rounded square (radius ≈ 22% of its size) carrying a stroked "Z" glyph (two horizontals joined by a diagonal, stroke 2.6/32 units, round caps, drawn in `--color-on-accent`). SVG source is in every mockup header (`.brand` block). Used at 28 px in headers, 20 px in compact bars, 56 px on the auth brand panel, and as the favicon (16/32 px). The wordmark is the seal + `Zen` (Shippori Mincho 700, `--color-ink-strong`) + `PDF` (Shippori Mincho 500, `--color-ink-muted`). Never the 🧘‍♀️ emoji, never typed-with-an-emoji.
- **The hanko stamp (completion).** Class `.stamp`: 2 px vermilion border + 1.5 px inset inner border (`box-shadow: inset 0 0 0 1.5px` at 45% accent), radius 6 px, Shippori Mincho 700, letter-spacing .22em, uppercase, rotated −4°, 7% accent wash fill. Appears **only when something finished**: a merged/converted result ("MERGED", "DONE"), a completed signature ("SIGNED"), a fresh upload's card ("JUST UPLOADED", small variant `.stamp-sm`). Entrance: scale 1.08→1 over 200 ms (static under reduced motion). Never used as a label, tag, or decoration.
- **The folded sheet.** Class `.sheet`: raised paper with a folded top-end corner (16 px CSS triangle). Reserved for trust/retention notices and empty states — at most one per screen.

### Voice & microcopy
Calm, plain, honest. States facts, never shouts, never upsells. Sentence case everywhere; no exclamation marks in chrome. Examples (use these verbatim where they fit):
1. "Files delete automatically after 24 hours."
2. "Merging… 3 of 5 pages"
3. "Everything already works without an account. An account is for keeping things."
4. "Free, paid for by advertising."
5. "Two files became one."
6. "Drop PDFs here, or click to browse"
7. "You were the last one — sealing the document now."
Processing copy breathes (progress counts pages/steps); errors say what happened and what to do next; the retention promise is surfaced proudly, not hidden.

### Typography
- **Display: Shippori Mincho** — weights 500, 600, 700. Humanist Mincho serif; carries the "ink & paper" character in Latin as well as it does in Japanese. OFL (Google Fonts), self-host woff2, `font-display: swap`; metric fallback Georgia.
- **UI: Zen Kaku Gothic New** — weights 400, 500, 700. Quiet, highly legible gothic sans, chosen (over a default grotesque) because its round terminals sit naturally beside a Mincho. OFL, self-host woff2, `font-display: swap`; fallback `-apple-system, "Segoe UI", system-ui, sans-serif` with `size-adjust` tuned at implementation.
- Roles: h1 display 600 at 34 px/1.25 (the landing h1 is the header masthead — §3); h2 display 600 22 px; h3 display 600 17 px; body UI 400 15 px/1.6; small 13.5 px; caption/hints 12.5 px; kicker UI 500 11 px, tracking .14em, uppercase, `--color-ink-faint`. Minimum text size anywhere: 11 px (kickers/legal footers only).

### Layout rhythm
Spacing scale above (4-based). Container widths: **marketing 1120 px**, **tool pages 720 px**, **reading (legal) 640 px**, **app shell full-width with 32 px gutters** (16 px under 768 px). Grid gaps: cards 12–16 px, page thumbnails 20 px. Radii: inputs/buttons 6 px, cards 10 px, sheets/page thumbs 2–4 px. Borders are hairline 1 px; 2 px only for emphasis (focus, selection, stamp).

### Icon grid
24×24 viewBox, 20×20 live area (2 px padding), stroke 1.5, round caps and joins, no fills (single exception: the redact bar and star-when-starred fill with `currentColor`), corner radius 1 on document shapes. Icons inherit `currentColor`; default color `--color-ink-muted`. All 24 tool icons are drawn on this grid — 24 reference SVGs ship in the mockups (see the landing directory); any new icon must be derivable from this spec.

### Z-index layers
`0` content · `10` sticky bars · `20` popover menus · `30` banners · `40` modals + scrim · `50` toasts.

### Breakpoints
`sm 640` · `md 768` · `lg 1024` · `xl 1280` (dashboard rail appears ≥ xl, as today) · `2xl 1440`.

## 3. Component specs

All values are for both modes via semantic tokens. Every interactive element keeps its existing `data-test` attribute.

### Buttons (`.btn`)
Base: UI 500 15 px, min-height 44 px (compact `.btn-sm` 36 px, never under 32 px), padding 0 20 px, radius 6 px, transitions `--dur-1`.
- **Primary** (one per screen, the screen's real action): bg `--color-accent`, text `--color-on-accent`. Hover `--color-accent-hover`; active `--color-accent-active`; loading: 14 px currentColor ring spinner + progressive label ("Merging…"). **This includes the ceremony's final CTA and the request builder's "Send it"** — the green/lavender variants are retired (defect D1).
- **Secondary**: bg `--color-surface-raised`, 1 px `--color-border-strong`, text `--color-ink`. Hover: border `--color-ink-faint`.
- **Ghost**: transparent, text `--color-ink-muted`; hover bg `--color-surface-raised` (dark: `--color-surface`).
- **Destructive**: transparent, 1 px `--color-danger` border, text `--color-danger`; hover bg `--color-danger-surface`. Filled `--color-danger` + white only on final confirmation inside a confirm panel.
- **Disabled (all variants, one treatment — defect D5)**: transparent bg, 1 px **dashed** `--color-border-strong` border, text `--color-ink-muted` (6.3:1 light / 8.8:1 dark — comfortably ≥3:1), `cursor: not-allowed`. Unmistakably distinct from every enabled variant (dashed hairline vs solid fill/border).
- **Focus-visible (everything interactive)**: `outline: 2px solid var(--color-focus); outline-offset: 2px`.

### Inputs & validation
Min-height 44 px, padding 0 14 px, radius 6 px, bg `--color-surface-raised`, 1 px `--color-border-strong`; placeholder `--color-ink-faint`; hover border `--color-ink-faint`; focus = focus ring (border transparent); disabled: bg `--color-bg`, dashed border, text `--color-ink-muted`. Invalid: border `--color-danger` + `aria-invalid` + error line 13 px `--color-danger` below (always in the DOM for screen readers where the code already does this). Hints 12.5 px `--color-ink-faint`. Password fields get a 44×44 px visibility toggle inside the field (eye icon) and an inline rules hint (defect D6). **Compact field (`.input-compact`)** *(added 2026-08-24)*: 36 px instead of 44, for the fields that take §6's dense-toolbar exemption — a page offset, a DPI, a recipient's order number, all of them in a rail or a dense row beside something else. **Below `md` it is 44 again**, because below `md` a rail is a bottom sheet (§3 Phone workspace) and the exemption stops applying. It exists because `.input` sets `min-height: 44px` in an unlayered stylesheet, which beats every Tailwind utility, so fourteen templates had written `!min-h-9` to get a shorter field — an `!important` in a template that only another `!important` could ever answer. A named pattern is the way out of that fight, not a wider `!`. Checkboxes/radios/ranges: `accent-color: var(--color-accent)` — a radio in the browser's default blue is the one colour the palette does not contain.

### Choice rows (`.cfield` as a radio/checkbox row)
Where a choice needs a sentence of explanation, it is a stacked list of `.cfield` rows rather than a `.seg` — `.seg` is a 36 px dense-toolbar control and these appear on marketing and tool pages, where the floor is 44 px. Each row: `<label class="cfield">` wrapping a native `<input type=radio|checkbox>` (real form semantics, keyboard and screen reader for free) + a 14 px `--color-ink` line + an optional 12.5 px `--color-ink-faint` sub-line; padding 12/14, hairline `--color-border`, radius 6 px, bg `--color-surface-raised`. Selected row: `.on` (border `--color-accent`) — the radio dot alone is not enough of a signal at a glance. Group caption uses `.cfield-label` inside a `<fieldset>`/`<legend>`; rows sit 8 px apart.

### Cards
- **Tool card (landing)**: icon (24 px, `--color-ink-muted`) + name (UI 500 15 px), padding 16/18, bg `--color-surface`, hairline border, radius 10 px. Hover: border `--color-ink-faint`, name `--color-ink-strong`. No sublabels — the group heading carries context (defect D4).
- **File card (dashboard)**: radius 10 px, padding 12, thumbnail 3:4 white with `--color-page-shadow`, checkbox top-start, ⋯ menu top-end (menu: raised surface, shadow-3, existing actions only: Rename / Download / Trash, or Restore / Delete forever in trash), star toggle (accent when starred), title UI 500 14 px, meta 12 px faint (`N pages · N MB`, lock glyph if encrypted). **A just-uploaded file appears once, as its grid card wearing a transient "JUST UPLOADED" `.stamp-sm` for ~6 s (fade 300 ms; no separate confirmation list — defect D2).** Upload *progress* rows may exist only while the upload is in flight and must disappear on completion.

### Dropzone
"Paper laid on a desk": bg `--color-surface-raised`, 1 px **dashed** `--color-border-strong`, radius 4 px, padding 48/24 (compact 24), sheet-with-arrow icon 36 px, prompt UI 500 15 px, hint 12.5 px faint. Drag-over: border solid `--color-accent`, bg `--color-accent-surface` (transition `--dur-2` — the sheet "receives" the paper). Whole zone is the click target and a labelled file input.

### Progress / processing
- **The breath**: 34 px circle, 2 px `--color-accent` border, scaling .82→1 with opacity .55→1 over 2.6 s ease-in-out, beside counting copy ("Merging… 3 of 5 pages"). Under reduced motion the circle is static at full scale and the counter alone carries progress. No frantic spinners anywhere.
- **Meter**: 6 px track `--color-bg` + hairline, fill `--color-accent`, radius 3 px (storage, upload %).

### Toasts
Bottom-end stack, max-width 360 px, raised surface + hairline + shadow-3, radius 6 px, 3 px inline-start rule: success `--color-success`, error `--color-danger`, info `--color-ink-faint`. Text 14 px ink. Click dismisses (as today); `role=alert` on errors, `status` otherwise. No colored fills — toasts are ink on paper with a colored spine.

**A session that ends on its own says so, in a toast** *(added 2026-08-24)*. When a
credential is refused and cannot be renewed, the client clears it — and until now did that
in silence, so the page carried on looking signed in and the person discovered it at the
next guarded route, bounced to **register** wearing the chrome of somebody who has never
had an account. It is a toast by this section's own rule: something that just happened and
can be missed. **Info tone, error dwell** — nothing is broken and nobody did anything
wrong, but it is a sentence to read and act on rather than a receipt. Copy:
*"Your session ended. Please sign in again."*

**And it moves them only where the page has actually stopped working.** Only the routes
`accountGuard` gates — dashboard, settings, the signing surfaces — redirect, and to
**login** carrying `next`, because a person who *had* a session has an account and should
land back where they were. Everything else stays put: every public route works without an
account, `/app/doc/:id` renders for either principal, and sending any of them to a login
form would be precisely the login wall §10 forbids. A stale token is not a reason to
interrupt somebody reading a document or confirming an email address.

### Inline notice (`.notice`) *(added 2026-08-23)*
A standing fact about the record on screen, stated where it applies: "the signed copy could not be added to your document", "this document cannot be deleted, and why". Flex row, wraps; padding 12/16, radius 6 px, hairline `--color-border-strong` with the **inline-start border at 3 px** as the tone rule (logical property — it follows the reading direction), bg `--color-surface-raised`, text UI 400 13.5 px `--color-ink`. Tone modifiers set the border and the surface only: `.notice-success` / `.notice-warning` / `.notice-danger` / `.notice-info` → `--color-{tone}` + `--color-{tone}-surface`.

**The tone never carries the words.** `--color-ink` on every tone surface measures ≥ 10.7:1 in light and ≥ 11.4:1 in dark; the tone colour on 13.5 px body copy would be the wrong side of §1's "colour is almost never the differentiator", and the rule is what tells you which kind it is. Tone-on-surface as a rule/border measures 4.56:1 (warning, light) to 7.54:1 (info, dark) — clear of the 3:1 UI floor in both modes.

**When it is a notice and not something else.** A **toast** is for something that just happened and can be missed; a notice is for a condition somebody may arrive at an hour later and must still find. A **`.sheet`** is one-per-screen and reserved for trust/retention notices and empty states. An **error line under an input** belongs to that input. If the sentence explains why a control is missing or a record is in an unusual state, it is a notice, and it sits next to the thing it is about — not at the top of the page and not in a toast that fades. Actions the sentence refers to (a download, a link) sit inside it, so the explanation and the way out are one object.

### Modals
Scrim `--color-scrim`; dialog raised surface, radius 10 px, shadow-3, padding 24, max-width 28 rem (signature pad 32 rem); title display 600 17 px; actions end-aligned, primary last. Esc closes (existing `zenModal` behavior). The **shortcuts sheet** is a standard 28 rem modal whose list scrolls inside itself as a focusable labelled region (`tabindex="0" role="region"`) — `.modal` is a scroll container and Safari does not make scrollers keyboard-focusable, so inside a focus trap a long list would otherwise be unreachable. Key caps use `.kbd`, each carrying the key's spoken name as an `aria-label` (a bare ⌘ is announced as "place of interest sign").

### Empty states
`.sheet` (folded corner), centered, icon 24 px faint, title display 500 16 px, subtitle 13.5 px muted. No emoji.

### Tabs / segmented workspace nav (`.seg`)
Row of 36 px text buttons, radius 6 px; idle `--color-ink-muted`; hover bg `--color-bg`; **selected: bg `--color-ink-strong`, text `--color-bg`** (ink stamp, not accent — accent stays reserved). Used for workspace modes, signature-pad tabs, job filters (badge-sized variant), and the annotate page bar's tick-mark selector *(2026-08-28)*.

**Semantics — toggle buttons, not ARIA tabs.** Each is a plain `<button>` carrying `aria-pressed`; the row is a `<div role="group">` with an `aria-label`. Never `aria-selected` (invalid on `role=button`) and never `role="tablist"` (it obliges every child to be a `role=tab`, which then needs `aria-controls`, `role=tabpanel` and roving tabindex). Both mistakes shipped once and were caught only after an earlier contrast failure stopped masking them — every `.seg` button stays individually tabbable.

### Tool palette button (`Annotate`) *(added 2026-08-23)*
The annotate rail's tool grid — two columns, 4 px gap — is a set of mutually exclusive toggles, and it follows `.seg`'s **semantics** (a plain `<button>` per tool carrying `aria-pressed`; never `aria-selected`, never `role="tablist"`) with `.seg`'s **selected treatment** (bg `--color-ink-strong`, text `--color-bg` — the ink stamp, not accent). Idle: hairline `--color-border`, radius 4 px, `--color-ink`, text start-aligned. It is **not** a `.seg`: `.seg`'s 36 px is the dense-desktop-toolbar exemption in §6, and this rail is a full-width section on a phone (§3 workspace panes) where these buttons are the only way to choose a tool. **Minimum height 44 px**, like the popover menu and for the same reason.

**A tool armed by something other than the palette still lights up in it.** Uploading a custom stamp arms `image_stamp`, so the palette carries an `image_stamp` entry: **disabled until a stamp exists in the session**, taking §3's single disabled treatment (dashed `--color-border-strong` hairline, `--color-ink-muted`, `cursor: not-allowed`) with a `title` saying what would enable it; once a stamp is uploaded it wears that image as a **24 px icon** (`object-contain`, hairline `--color-border`, `--radius-1`) beside its label, and clicking it re-arms the tool. Native `disabled` is correct here — unlike a `role="menuitem"` (§3 context menu), a disabled `<button>` keeps its place in the accessibility tree. The icon is decorative (`alt=""`): the button's own words are its name. The rule generalises: **an editing mode may not enter a state its own palette cannot show or return to** — that is the no-dead-affordances invariant read from the other side.

### Tick box (Annotate) *(added 2026-08-28)*
The palette's eighteenth entry, for ticking a printed form's checkboxes without drawing each mark by hand. Arming it puts a **mark selector** in the annotate page bar, after Undo/Redo: a `.seg` (§3 tabs — the page bar is the dense desktop toolbar §6's 36 px exemption is for, and the bar *wraps* rather than hiding it, per §3 workspace panes) of three toggle buttons — **✓ Check**, the default; **− Dash**; **✗ Cross** — carrying `aria-pressed`, each glyph `aria-hidden` beside its word so nothing is announced twice. The selector renders only while the tool is armed: it answers a question only this tool asks, exactly as the stamp picker shows only for Stamp.

**Where the bar wraps, measured** *(2026-08-28, the CLI's browser pass — this paragraph replaces a "below `md`" that was written from the phone rule and was wrong about the desk)*. The selector is 229 px, so whether the bar takes one row or two is a question about the **pane**, not the breakpoint: with the comments rail open the annotate pane is 816 px at a 1280 px window and the bar goes **53 → 97 px** while the tool is armed, putting the unsaved badge, Save and Flatten on a second row; at 1440 (pane 976) and 1600 (pane 1136) it stays one row. That is the wrap working — nothing is clipped, hidden or overlaid, and the second row costs 44 px of page only while the tool is armed — and it is the reason the selector is **not** allowed to become a scroller or to hide behind a "more" affordance: a control that answers *which mark am I about to place* must be readable at the moment of placing it.

One click places the chosen mark, centred on the click as a **14 pt square in page points** — square on paper, not on screen — clamped so a click at the page's edge keeps the whole mark on the page; and the tool **stays armed**, because ticking a form is *click, click, click*. The mark is **plain ink strokes**, deliberately not a glyph in a text box: strokes depend on no font's coverage, render identically on the overlay and in the saved file, and the placed mark selects, drags, resizes, copies and deletes like any other drawing — in the file and the comments rail it *is* ink. Colour, line width and opacity come from the ink family's own controls (§3 Text on the page); the chosen mark is remembered for the session the way the families keep their colours, and a fresh Annotate starts at ✓.

### Footer (`.ftr`)

Separators are a middot with a space either side, in the flow of a single `<p>` — so the row **wraps as text, not as a grid**. What is specified is the behaviour, not the break point: the row may occupy as many centred lines as it needs, the links stay in their fixed order across the wrap, and **the page never scrolls horizontally at any width**. Each link keeps a 10 px inline margin either side, which gives a wrapped line its own breathing room without a second rule.

A wrapped line ends with its middot — "… · E-sign disclosure ·" — and that is **accepted, not overlooked**. A middot-separated text row has to put the separator on one side of the break or the other; binding it to the following link merely moves the oddity to the start of the next line. The only layout that avoids it entirely is a gap-based flex row with no middots at all, and that would restyle the five-link footer the contract has pinned since 2026-08-06 — a bigger change than adding two links, and not one Phase 11 was sanctioned to make. *(Recorded 2026-08-24 after measuring: the first draft of this paragraph claimed no separator is ever stranded, which the implementation immediately disproved at 390 px. ### Workspace panes (`.ws-panes` / `.ws-rail` / `.ws-pane-main`) *(added 2026-08-20)*
Every workspace mode is the same three-part figure: a start rail, the page, sometimes an end rail. `.ws-panes` is the row; `.ws-rail` a rail (`--color-surface`, hairline on the inline edge that faces the page — `.ws-rail-start` / `.ws-rail-end` — width set per mode as today); `.ws-pane-main` the page pane, which **must** carry `min-width: 0` so a wide page scrolls inside its own pane rather than widening the row.

**Below `md` the rails become drawers and the mode nav becomes a bottom bar** — see **Phone workspace** below. *(Amended 2026-08-24. From 2026-08-20 until then the panes **stacked**: rails became full-width sections above and below the page, each capped at `34vh`. That made every control reachable and stopped the page scrolling sideways, and it was, in the queue row's own words, a rescue rather than a design — at 390 px the tool palette owned the top third of the screen, the page bar wrapped to two rows, and the document began 600 px down a 844 px screen. The stacking rules are gone; nothing may be left behind that no width can reach.)*

**Desktop (≥ `md`) is unchanged by that amendment, and that is an invariant.** The three-part figure above is the desktop layout and stays exactly as it is — same DOM, same widths, same hairlines, same toolbars in the same places. A phone treatment that costs the desk anything is the wrong phone treatment.

**Nothing is hidden at a breakpoint** — a control you cannot reach on a phone is a dead affordance with extra steps. What the amendment permits is a control *moving*: a rail's contents move into that rail's drawer, and the pane's own Undo / Redo / primary move into the bottom bar. Moved is not hidden; the test is whether the person can still get to it, and in how many taps. **The same rule governs the right-click menu** *(added 2026-08-21)*: right-click does not exist on a touch screen, so every action the menu offers must also be a real control in a rail — the list of marked redaction areas, of signature placements, of request fields, and the per-item actions on a comment row. The menu is an accelerator, never the mechanism. On a phone the rail *is* a drawer, so "reachable in a rail" means **one tap from the bottom bar**, and the bottom bar therefore carries an opener for every rail the current mode has.

The **page's own render width is measured from the pane it is drawn in** — the scroller's own content box, via a `ResizeObserver` (`shared/fit-width.ts`), capped at each mode's desk width — never fixed, so the first paint fits and a turned phone re-fits. *(Amended 2026-08-24. It used to say "seeded from the viewport (`min(900, innerWidth − 48)`)", and only Annotate did even that: **Edit and Forms drew the page at 750 px, Protect and Sign at 680, and Compare at 420 × 2 + a 16 px gap — inside a pane 327 px wide** — and five of the six have no zoom control, so the page could not be brought into view at all. The viewport was the wrong number twice over: it does not know about the rails, which take 466 px of a desk in Annotate, and it does not count the scrollbar the *pane* has. Only the element knows.)*

**Below `md` that scroller reserves its scrollbar gutter** *(added 2026-08-24, after the above shipped)*. Measuring the pane makes the page's width depend on the pane, and the page's width sets its height, and the height decides whether the pane needs a vertical scrollbar — which, where the browser draws a **classic** one, changes the content box by 15 px and feeds back in. Where the page is *just* tall enough, two widths satisfy that loop and one of them does not fit: measured on production at 390 px in a **guest** workspace — whose pane is 525 px tall against a signed-in 621, because a guest carries the account CTA — **Edit and Sign settled at 342 with `scrollWidth` 390 against `clientWidth` 375, while Annotate and Protect settled at 327**, same document, same session. Reserving the gutter removes the coupling instead of damping it: one content box, one fixed point. **Below `md` only**, because §10 says the desk does not pay for the phone and the desk has one fixed point already — its page is far taller than its pane at either candidate width, so the scrollbar is needed regardless. On a real phone it costs nothing: touch scrollbars are overlays and the reserved gutter is 0 px. The 15 px exists only in a desktop browser at a narrow window, which is also the only place the defect exists.

**In-pane toolbars wrap; they never overflow.** *(added 2026-08-21, in the follow-ups that landed as PR #19 — the amendment was committed undated)* The row above each pane (page nav, zoom, undo/redo, the save cluster; the organize operations; the find bar) carries `flex-wrap` — as the organize toolbar always has. Only one of the seven did, and Annotate's needs 609 px against a 390 px phone, so the row spilled and dragged the *document* sideways with it; Chrome then widened its layout viewport to 609 and drew the whole app at about 64 %. Wrapping rather than scrolling, because these rows end in Save — a primary action that must not be hidden off an edge. The workspace bar is the deliberate exception: it scrolls (§3 headers), because nine mode buttons wrapped would push the page down on every screen.

`flex-wrap` still governs tablets, and below `md` it has less to wrap: **Phone workspace** takes the pane's Undo / Redo and its primary out of this row and docks them in the bottom bar. What is left fits one line in six of the seven modes. Annotate is the seventh — page nav, zoom, the unsaved badge and **Flatten** measure 437 px against 374, so it takes two, and that is the rule working rather than failing. Flatten is not hoisted with Save: the bar docks a mode's *primary*, and a second dock button would crowd out the one that matters.

### Phone workspace (`.ws-bottom-bar` / `.ws-drawer`) *(added 2026-08-24)*
Below `md`, `/app/doc/:id` is three things and their order is the whole design: **the page first**, a **persistent bottom bar**, and the rails as **drawers over the page**. Nothing above `md` changes.

**Page first.** The page pane is the first thing under the workspace bar and takes every pixel the two bars do not. Nothing opens over it on arrival: **no mode opens its drawer by default, and that includes Annotate.** The person navigated to a document; covering it with a panel before they have seen it is answering a question they did not ask, and every panel is one 44 px tap away. First paint is fit-to-width (D7, §4) at 390 px as at 1280, and the width comes from **the pane, not the viewport** — measured at 390 as **342 px** where scrollbars are overlays and 327 where the browser draws a classic one, which is the second number and the reason the window is not asked. Measure the pane; do not assume the viewport.

**Bottom bar** — the last row of the workspace's full-height column, `bg-surface`, hairline `--color-border` on the block-start edge, `--shadow-up-3` (§2), **layer 10** (§2 sticky bars), and `padding-block-end: env(safe-area-inset-bottom)` so the home indicator never sits on a control. It is a flex child rather than `position: fixed` — the column already fills the viewport and never scrolls, so the bar is persistent by construction and nothing has to reserve space under it. Two rows, and the second is always there:

- **Action row (`.ws-bottom-actions`)**, 52 px, always present — every mode has at least one rail and therefore at least one opener. At the start, one **drawer opener** per rail the mode has (`.btn-ghost`, 44 px, the drawer's own title as its label, `aria-expanded` + `aria-controls`). Docked at the end, the **mode's own Undo / Redo pair** and its **primary action** where the pane has one. Convert has none of the three and its dock is empty: it offers OCR, an export and a repair as peers, and a disabled Save invented to fill the row would be a dead affordance. Phase 12 put an Undo/Redo pair in every editing surface's page bar; on a phone that pair belongs in the bar, because the alternative is a person hunting for it in a wrapped toolbar above a page they are trying to look at. The pane's copies in the page bar are not drawn twice — below `md` they are the ones that give way (see **In-pane toolbars wrap** above).
- **Mode row (`.ws-bottom-modes`)**, 56 px, the nine modes as **icon-over-label** buttons: 24 px icon on the §2 grid, 11 px UI 500 label, ≥ 44 px target, ≥ 56 px wide. The row **scrolls horizontally inside itself** — no wrap, no scrollbar, children never shrink — exactly as the workspace bar does and for the same reason: nine buttons wrapped would take a second row from the page. It is a `<div role="group">` with an `aria-label`, each child a plain `<button>` carrying `aria-pressed` — never `aria-selected`, never `role="tablist"` (§3 `.seg` semantics, which this row inherits along with the **selected treatment**: bg `--color-ink-strong`, text `--color-bg`, the ink stamp, not accent).

**Drawers (`.ws-drawer`).** A rail below `md` is a **bottom sheet**: `position: fixed`, full inline width, sitting on top of the bottom bar, `max-block-size: 62dvh`, `--color-surface`, hairline `--color-border`, `--shadow-3`, block-start corners at `--radius-3`, **layer 40** (§2 modals) over a `--color-scrim` at the same layer. A bottom sheet rather than a side drawer: a phone is held in one hand, the reachable third of the screen is the bottom, and a side drawer on a 390 px screen is a full-width panel that has simply arrived from the wrong direction.

- **One at a time, and the scrim is what enforces it.** With a sheet open the bottom bar is behind the scrim, so a second opener cannot be tapped at all — the way to another panel is to close this one, which is the same rule the focus trap applies to the keyboard. The facade also refuses to hold two, so nothing programmatic can. Two sheets on a 390 px screen is a stack of chrome with no page left under it.
- **The head** is sticky at the top of the sheet: a 36 × 4 px `--color-border-strong` **handle** (centred, `--radius-1`, `aria-hidden` — it is a grip, and grips are not read out), the drawer's **title** (display 600 17 px), and a 44 × 44 px **close** button at the end.
- **Escape closes it, a tap on the scrim closes it, and focus is trapped while it is open** — by the same CDK focus trap the modals use (§3 modals, `zenModal`), never hand-rolled. Focus returns to the opener, because the opener is where the person was.
- **Body scroll is locked** while a drawer is open. A sheet you can scroll the page behind is a sheet that moves when you try to use it.
- **Motion:** `transform: translateY` over `--dur-2` with `--ease`, and nothing else — no fade, no scale. Under `prefers-reduced-motion` the global collapse (§1) makes it instant, and that must remain *legible*: the sheet is fully drawn and fully positioned with the transition removed, so nothing about the drawer may be carried by the animation.
- **A sheet is not a dense desktop toolbar, so §6's 44 px floor applies inside it.** Every `<button>` in a drawer, its `.seg` tab row, the palette's colour swatch and sliders, and the row carrying a checkbox are all ≥ 44 px below `md`. Measured before the rule: the comment row's Copy / Duplicate / Delete — the controls D8 requires to be touch-reachable — were **16 px**, Edit's five mode toggles 30, and the tab rows 36. What keeps its platform size on purpose: a native checkbox and a native file input. *(Corrected 2026-08-24. This paragraph said three number/select fields kept `!min-h-9` because "an `!important` … only an `!important` could answer". The count was wrong — there were **fourteen** `!min-h-9` and twenty-one `!min-h-8` — and so was the attribution: the `!important` was fighting `.input`'s own unlayered `min-height: 44px` at every width, not any rule this section added. All fourteen now use `.input-compact` (§3 inputs), which is 36 px on a desk and 44 in a sheet. The twenty-one 32 px fields are a separate open row.)*
- **What lives in which drawer** is exactly what lived in which rail: the start rail is the mode's panel, the end rail is Annotate's comments. Phase 12's **Areas / Placements / Fields** lists and the comment rows' per-item actions are inside those panels and therefore one tap from the bottom bar, which is what D8 requires of them (see **Workspace panes** above).
- **The Annotate palette stays a drawer; it is deliberately not a compact bottom row.** Three reasons, recorded because the alternative was asked for: the palette is **eighteen** mutually exclusive tools *(seventeen when this was written; the Tick box joined 2026-08-28)* and only a handful have a glyph drawn on the §2 grid, so a row means either eighteen new icons — a bigger change than this one — or an unlabelled scroll of look-alikes; the same rail also carries colour, width, opacity, font size, the stamp picker and the custom-stamp upload, which are not a row at all; and a third fixed row at the bottom would leave a 390 × 844 screen with under half its height for the document. A labelled sheet you open beats a cryptic row you cannot avoid.

**The overflow sheet.** The workspace bar's own version Undo / Redo, Split, Compress, Download and Shortcuts move into a **More** drawer below `md` (§3 headers). It is a drawer and not a popover `.menu` on purpose: on a phone these are six full-width 44 px rows in the thumb's reach, and the drawer already carries the focus trap, the Escape and the scrim that a popover would have to be given again.

**Every existing `data-test` is kept** — a drawer *is* the same panel, it does not replace it. The additions are `ws-bottom-bar`, `ws-bottom-mode`, `ws-bottom-undo`, `ws-bottom-redo`, `ws-bottom-primary`, `ws-drawer`, `ws-drawer-open`, `ws-drawer-close`, `ws-drawer-scrim` and `ws-more`. **`ws-drawer` goes on the sheet's head, not on the rail**, for the reason parity exists: one rail already carries a `data-test` of its own (`comments-sidebar`), and a host binding would have overwritten it. The head is also the more honest assertion — it exists only while the rail is a drawer. The sheet itself is addressed by `data-ws-drawer="<key>"`, which is a hook and not a test id.

**The pane's Undo/Redo and primary appear once.** Below `md` the page bar's own pair is `display: none` (`.ws-hoisted`) and the bar draws the registered pair; a mode whose primary lives in its rail — Protect's *Protect document*, Sign's *Apply signature*, Edit's staged-text *Save* — keeps it there **and** publishes it to the bar, which is the same control offered from the two places a person would look, never two controls on screen at once (the drawer is over the bar, under the scrim).

### Context menu (`.menu`) *(added 2026-08-21)*
The one popover pattern in the product, shared by the dashboard's ⋯ menu and the right-click menu over a page. Raised surface, hairline `--color-border`, `shadow-3`, radius 6 px, 4 px padding, `min-width: 160px`, **layer 20** (§2) — and it must be given `position: absolute` at the call site, because a `z-index` on a static box does nothing.

**Items are 44 px tall.** §6's 36 px exemption is for dense *desktop toolbars*; a popover is neither, and on a touch screen these rows are the only way to reach some actions at all. Item text is UI 400 14 px `--color-ink`; hover and the keyboard's current entry (`.is-active`) both take `--color-surface` + `--color-ink-strong`. Removals use `.menu-danger` (`--color-danger`, `--color-danger-surface` on hover/active) and are last. An optional end column `.menu-key` shows the shortcut in 12.5 px `--color-ink-faint`; `.menu-sep` is a hairline between groups.

**Unavailable entries carry `aria-disabled="true"`, never the native `disabled` attribute** — a natively disabled button drops out of the accessibility tree, so "Paste (unavailable)" vanishes instead of reading as unavailable. They take §3's single disabled treatment: dashed `--color-border-strong` hairline, `--color-ink-muted`, `cursor: not-allowed`.

**Keyboard and roles.** `role="menu"` with an `aria-label` naming what it belongs to; items `role="menuitem"` with a **roving tabindex** (one `0`, the rest `-1`). `↑`/`↓` cycle and skip disabled entries, `Home`/`End` jump, `Enter`/`Space` activate, `Tab` closes, `Esc` closes and returns focus to whatever opened it. The menu **stops propagation on every key it handles**, and the surface underneath ignores the keyboard entirely while it is open — otherwise walking the menu also nudges the shape beneath it. Its own focus ring is drawn *inside* the item (`outline-offset: -2px`), because 2 px of ring at 2 px of offset crosses the menu's 4 px padding. The current entry is highlighted from the component's own index, **not** `:focus-visible`: a menu opened by a right-click is a pointer interaction, and programmatic focus straight after one does not match `:focus-visible` in any engine.

**An empty action list means no menu** — the browser's own is left alone rather than replaced with an empty box (§10, no dead UI). And per §3 workspace panes, an action offered on right-click must also be reachable from a rail: right-click does not exist on a phone.

**Placement over a page is physical** (`left`/`top`), clamped inside the page box so the scrolling pane cannot clip it. See §8 for why this one case is not logical.

### Text on the page (`.page-text`) *(added 2026-08-20)*
A text annotation is drawn **inside its own rectangle**, in the annotation's colour, at its point size converted against the page's real width in points (`fontSize / pageWidthPt × renderWidth`) — never as a badge outside the box, and never truncated. Wrapping is `pre-wrap` + `overflow-wrap: anywhere`, line-height 1.25, clipped to the rect.

Editing happens in the same box: `.page-text-editing` swaps the drawn text for a textarea of identical metrics, focused on creation and on double-click, with a 1.5 px `--color-accent` ring, an accent caret and a paper-coloured wash (the page raster underneath is white in both themes, so the wash is invisible on empty paper and readable over dense content). It commits **on blur**, not per keystroke, so one undo takes back a sentence. `Esc` and `⌘/Ctrl+Enter` end editing; plain `Enter` is a new line. A box left empty is removed rather than left on the page as an invisible, unselectable rectangle.

**The next gesture on the page ends the box being typed into, and ends it first** *(amended 2026-08-26)*. Filling in a form is *type, draw the next box, type* — and that path never blurred: a drawing gesture cancels `pointerdown`, a cancelled pointerdown moves no focus, and the editor was torn down uncommitted when the new box took over. What happened then was the browser's choice — Chromium reports `blur` for a removed element and the report was read against the *new*, empty box, which was deleted (every second box vanished as it was drawn); WebKit and Firefox report nothing and the words were lost (the *previous* box vanished — still there, empty, invisible). So the overlay commits the open box itself, synchronously, at the start of any pointer gesture on the page, and an end-of-editing **names the box it is about**; the empty-box rule above is applied to that box and never to whichever one is being edited now. Undo, redo and *Edit text…* close the open box the same way before they re-target it. The commit-on-blur rule stands for every other way out — `Esc`, `⌘/Ctrl+Enter`, a click anywhere else.

**A drawn text box is never shorter than one line of its type** *(amended 2026-08-26, later the same day)*. Tracing a printed form line yields a drag a few points tall — thinner than the type about to go into it — and because the box clips to its rectangle, on screen and in the file alike, every such box showed the top half of its words and read as "all the text is cropped". At creation a drag shorter than **one line of its type at the current zoom** — `⌈1.25 × the font's pixels⌉ + 2 px` for the box's insets, measured in the overlay's own pixels and taken back to the page, because the insets are device pixels and a multiple of the point size that held at 900 px was a pixel short below about 660 *(corrected 2026-08-26, later)* — is grown to that height **upwards** — the traced line is the one the words are written *on*, so the box's bottom edge stays where the drag ended and its top moves up — clamped to the page; a drag that was tall enough is kept exactly as drawn, and the person can still resize either way afterwards. With it, the **resize handles are rendered only while the Select tool is armed**: they are Select's affordance, and under a draw tool they used to stay live while the items beneath them did not, so a box drawn just below the selected one — routine once boxes are a full line tall on a form whose rows are shorter — grabbed its bottom handle and resized it instead. **Sign and the signature-request builder keep their handles under their draw tool** *(2026-08-26, later)*: their `rect` is a placement tool the mode never leaves — a signature, once made, stays; the builder arms its first signer on entry — so a placement has no Select tool to fall back to and is sized by its handles where it was placed (`handlesWhileDrawing` on the overlay, set by those two surfaces only). The file matches the screen, because the rectangle is grown before the annotation is created rather than only where it is drawn.

**The Annotate raster is the page without its annotations.** The overlay draws every annotation itself — that is what makes them selectable, movable, editable — so its page image is requested clean (`?annots=false`), or each saved mark shows twice: baked into the raster at PyMuPDF's metrics and drawn on top at the overlay's, and the baked one stays behind when the other is dragged. That doubling is what "the text copies itself" looked like, and it appeared on every Save and every 30-second autosave. *Clean* means without the page's markup, **not** without its form fields: a filled form's widgets are still drawn, because nothing in the overlay draws those. Edit, Forms, Protect and Sign keep the full raster — it is the only place a highlight or a comment shows in those modes.

The working colour and opacity are held **per tool family** — `markup` (highlighter yellow, 70%) and `ink` (`#332D24`, 100%) — and switch with the tool, each family remembering whatever the user last chose for it. One shared swatch is what drew text boxes in highlighter yellow with a 2 pt yellow frame.

## 5. Theming implementation notes

- `.dark` on `<html>`; semantic custom properties re-declared under `.dark` (see §2). Components never reference primitives or raw hex.
- **Toggle**: icon button (sun / moon / monitor at 20 px on the 1.5-stroke grid), ≥44 px hit target, in **every** header variant including ceremony and auth. Cycles Light → Dark → System (default System). `aria-label` announces current state ("Theme: Dark — change theme"). Persist under `localStorage["zenpdf.theme"]` with values `light|dark|system`.
- **FOUC prevention**: a classic, non-deferred `<head>` script (before any stylesheet paint) on the prerendered pages: read the key, resolve `system` via `matchMedia('(prefers-color-scheme: dark)')`, set the class synchronously. Also listen for system changes while `system` is active. It must be an **external same-origin file** (`public/theme-boot.js`), never an inline block — production serves `script-src 'self' 'wasm-unsafe-eval'`, so inline script is refused and the theme is silently never applied before hydration. For the same reason the production build sets `styles.inlineCritical: false`: Angular's critical-CSS inlining ships the stylesheet as `media="print"` with an inline `onload` handler to activate it, and that handler is an inline event handler the CSP also refuses — which left the entire non-critical stylesheet, `.dark` tokens included, inert in production. Neither may be reintroduced without adding a CSP hash for it.
- **`color-scheme` follows the theme**: `:root { color-scheme: light }`, `.dark { color-scheme: dark }` (added 2026-08-18). Native controls — radio, checkbox, scrollbar, caret, autofill — read this and nothing in the token layer, so without it the dark theme drew them from the light palette: an unchecked radio rendered as a solid white disc, indistinguishable from a checked one.
- `<meta name="theme-color">` per mode: light `#F5F1E6`, dark `#171310` (swap on toggle; both emitted with `media` attrs for prerender).
- PDF canvas: backdrop `--color-canvas-backdrop` responds to mode; pages stay white; in dark mode add the hairline page border. The viewer shell (`ngx-extended-pdf-viewer` frame/toolbar) is restyled via its CSS variables/theme hooks to surface + ink tokens — shell and visibility rules only, no pixel re-skin of internals.
- **Two things the visibility half of that rule now does** *(added 2026-08-24)*. `#editorModeButtons` is hidden outright: the seven `show*Editor` inputs already turn the annotation editors off, but the library implements "off" by putting `.invisible` on the *inner* `<button>`, while `role="radio"` is a static attribute on the `<pdf-shy-button>` **host** — and Angular writes static attributes to the host as well as binding them. Six `role="radio"` nodes with no `aria-checked`, inside a `role="radiogroup"`, were sitting in the accessibility tree around six buttons nobody could see. And the library renumbers every focusable element under the viewer with a **positive** tabindex — measured, **152 of them, 1 to 152** — which are all visited before any `tabindex="0"` in the document, so a keyboard user met 152 vendor controls before the back link. `ViewerTabOrder` flattens the positives to 0 and leaves `-1` alone. Both are labelled workarounds and go when upstream fixes them.
- **What is deliberately not done**: pdf.js's zoom buttons are 24 px and the library pulls them 2 px into each other, so `#primaryZoomOut` fails a 24 px target-size check and is the one Lighthouse audit still failing on `/app/doc/:id`. Overriding that margin would buy the tick and still leave a 24 px control — 20 px short of §6's floor — and reaching 44 px means changing the vendor toolbar's own `--toolbar-height`, which is the pixel re-skin this rule forbids. A change that clears an audit without clearing the contract's floor is the weakest kind of change to make against a rule. Recorded upstream instead.

- Focus: `:focus-visible` 2 px `--color-focus`, offset 2 px, everywhere; never removed, never browser default.
- Hit targets ≥44×44 px for all controls (compact 36 px buttons only inside dense desktop toolbars, with ≥8 px spacing).
- Reduced motion: the existing global collapse stays; the breath and stamp degrade to static as specified.
- Landmarks: one `<h1>` per page (tool pages keep their exact H1s); `header/nav/main/footer` on every screen; toasts remain live regions; disclosure box remains a focusable labelled region; error messages keep their always-present `role=alert` pattern.

- **No single-character shortcuts** *(added 2026-08-21)*. WCAG 2.1 SC 2.1.4 only permits a shortcut made of a letter, number, punctuation or symbol if it can be switched off, remapped, or is active on focus alone. Rather than build a remapping UI, every binding in the product carries a modifier or is a non-printable key — which is why the shortcuts sheet is `⌘/` and not `?`, and why zoom keeps its buttons and gains no key.

It is declarative only: it creates no binding, VoiceOver does not announce it and NVDA's support is partial, so it is never the only place a key is named. The shortcuts sheet is.

- Popover menu items are **44 px**; the 36 px compact exemption above is for dense desktop toolbars only. Menu roles, roving tabindex and focus return are specified in §3.

## 8. RTL & future localization

Logical properties only: `margin-inline-start`, `padding-inline-end`, `inset-inline-start`, `border-start-start-radius`, `text-align: start` — never left/right physical properties in new CSS.

**One deliberate exception, on the page raster only** *(added 2026-08-21)*: everything positioned *over a rendered PDF page* — the shape layer, the word boxes, the text box, the selection chrome, the context menu — is placed with physical `left`/`top`, and arrow-key nudging is physical too (`ArrowLeft` moves a shape left in every locale). The reason is that the thing underneath does not mirror: the page is an `<img>` raster and an SVG user-space layer, and the overlay measures the pointer physically (`clientX − box.left`). Feeding a physically-measured offset into `inset-inline-start` would fling the menu across the page under `dir="rtl"`, and a direction-relative arrow would move a shape the opposite way from the key that was pressed. Every direct-manipulation editor behaves this way. **This exception does not extend to layout CSS**, which stays logical everywhere including the rails and lists that sit beside the page. Layouts flip cleanly under `dir="rtl"`. Icon mirroring: directional icons (back arrow, extract, next/prev chevrons, the convert arrows) mirror in RTL; document, seal, stamp, lock, star and signature icons do not. The seal glyph "Z" never mirrors. Dates/numbers via locale-aware formatting when localization lands.
